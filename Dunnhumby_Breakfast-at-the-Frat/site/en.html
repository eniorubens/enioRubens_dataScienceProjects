Does modeling stores and products improve sales prediction?

In the final window, the multilevel model with random intercepts (C0) had lower overall error than the ordinary least squares (OLS) regressions. Among store–product pairs seen in training, GPBoost had a slight edge when each pair counted equally. C0 had lower error with equal weight per row and on unseen pairs.

How do price and promotion relate to sales?

The panel follows each store × UPC pair over several weeks. Under the null model, the estimated correlation between two weeks for the same pair was 75.4%. C0 allows sales levels to vary by store, product and pair. Price and discount slopes are shared across pairs.

Sensitivity to price and discount

In F3, the coefficient for the log ratio of paid price to base price was −1.685. The coefficient for within-pair variation in log base price was −1.592. With pair and week fixed effects, the estimates were similar (−1.703 and −1.598, respectively).

Promotion types

The model uses indicators for feature advertising, in-store display and shelf-tag-only temporary price reductions (TPR). In training, every TPR_ONLY week had a discount. The scenarios combine promotion type and discount depth within observed ranges.

The percentages give the estimated difference in units sold after converting from the F3 log scale. The counts come from notebook 03 and include training rows with the same promotion type and a discount of 20% ± 3 percentage points. The retailer's choice of when and where to promote can influence the estimates.

The coefficients describe conditional associations in weeks with recorded transactions. Measuring incremental impact to inform pricing decisions requires a causal design. Calculating ROI also requires margin data.

How P1 compared the models

After cleaning, the panel had 524,926 observations, 77 stores, 55 UPCs and 156 weeks. P1 set the order and selection rule before fitting the candidate models. The two validation folds used the first 104 weeks.

C1 reduced error for pairs with fewer than 26 weeks of training in both folds. Across all eligible pairs, the average gain was −0.49%. The protocol required gains in both folds and an average gain of at least 0.5%, so P1 selected C0. Whether random slopes would help pairs with limited history in the final window remains open.

Retrospective final window

P1 fitted C0 and the benchmarks on weeks 0–103. After freezing the specification, P1 evaluated weeks 104–155 (January 12, 2011 to January 4, 2012) once. The initial exploration, which included the OLS in the project brief, had already used the full panel.

Coverage and uncertainty

Weeks without a recorded transaction may reflect zero sales or a stockout. In training, residuals from consecutive weeks for the same pair had a correlation of 0.196. The within-pair sensitivity analysis used standard errors clustered by pair and week, which were larger than the F3 Wald standard errors.
