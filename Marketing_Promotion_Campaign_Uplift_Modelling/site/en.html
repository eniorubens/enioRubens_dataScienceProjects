01

Business question

Who should receive an email because it changes their outcome? Propensity predicts response; uplift estimates the difference between treatment and control.

02

Experiment and validation

Hillstrom: 64,000 customers randomized across three arms. Binary development pools either email versus control. Deterministic 60%/20%/20% split; primary outcome visit. Preregistered evaluation on 12,800 sealed customers with 2,000 paired bootstrap samples.

03

Confirmatory result

UpliftTree − baseline: Δ Qini AUC = −0.0088; 95% CI [−0.0492; 0.0302]. The interval includes zero: primary superiority was not demonstrated. X+Tree achieved absolute Qini 0.0470, but its difference against baseline also includes zero; it is not a retrospectively promoted winner.

04

Heterogeneity, policy and ROI

Later stages are exploratory and consume persisted outputs. Visit–spend Spearman = 0.0941: engagement is not a reliable revenue proxy. Policies use IPW and budget constraints. ROI relies on illustrative costs and margins and does not propagate full effect uncertainty.

05

Reusable architecture

Eight PT/EN notebook pairs share src/: data, features, splits, learners, evaluation, policies and reports. Notebooks communicate the analysis; reusable logic resides in modules. S6–S9 artifacts document results and limitations.

06

Decision and limitations

not_ready_for_direct_deployment. Evidence supports learning and designing a prospective randomized pilot. Before the pilot: freeze policy, prespecify outcome, assess power, use real costs and monitor fatigue, opt-outs, deliverability and capacity. Visits do not equal conversion or revenue.
