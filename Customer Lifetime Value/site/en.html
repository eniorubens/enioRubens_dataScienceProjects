Executive report · Customer Lifetime Value

Predict future value to prioritise relationships.

This project estimates each customer’s expected revenue over the next 180 days from transaction history, combining BG/NBD for future purchases and Gamma-Gamma for spend per transaction. The page summarises executed results and keeps the limits of revenue-based forecasting visible.

01 · OVERVIEW

From purchase history to expected value

The business question is simple: which customers deserve more attention when past value does not necessarily represent future value? The pipeline turns RFM into two probabilistic forecasts and then into a marketing-action matrix.

01 · RFMFrequency, recency, customer age and average transaction value.
02 · BG/NBDFuture purchases and the probability that a customer is still active.
03 · Gamma-GammaExpected spend per transaction with Bayesian shrinkage.
04 · CLTVThe product of both components over 180 days.

What the project delivers

An individual future-revenue forecast, segmentation by value and P(alive), and a Flask API for customer scoring.

What it does not deliver

It does not estimate margin, campaign causal effect or observed ROI. Projected revenue must be combined with real costs and controlled experimentation.

Main reading: future value is concentrated in a small customer group, but investment decisions also depend on activity probability and real contact costs.

02 · DATA AND PREPARATION

A transaction base reduced to RFM

The source is the UCI Online Retail dataset, covering non-contractual UK retail transactions from December 2010 to December 2011. The project removes missing customer identifiers, cancellations and non-economic values before building the customer-level matrix.

03 · TEMPORAL VALIDATION

Know the past before predicting the future

The notebook splits the period chronologically: the first 75% is used for calibration and the last 25% is used as holdout. The model is fitted only on calibration data and evaluated against later purchases from 1,901 customers.

Gamma-Gamma assumption: |r| < 0.3 holds in the calibration base (r = 0.1135; p < 0.001). This supports fitting the monetary component in this sample, but does not remove the need for monitoring in another time window.

Interpretation: the holdout validates population-level frequency prediction. It does not validate margin, campaign financial return or future segment stability.

04 · PROBABILISTIC MODELS

Two models, two different uncertainties

BG/NBD

Models individual purchase frequency while a customer is active and the probability of permanent dropout. The materialised full MAP fit produced r = 1.5829, alpha = 85.0371, a = 0.0294 and b = 0.9707.

Main outputs: expected purchases over the horizon and P(alive).

Gamma-Gamma

Estimates expected spend per transaction and shrinks extreme averages for customers with few observations. The materialised MAP fit produced p = 1.9806, q = 4.1655 and v = 621.0724.

Main output: expected value per repeat transaction.

Operational formula: CLTV(t) = expected purchases in t days × expected spend per transaction. The executed notebook uses t = 180 days; the code also exposes MCMC fitting for posterior uncertainty, but this published result is the materialised MAP fit.

05 · ACTIONABLE SEGMENTATION

Future value combined with reachability

06 · GOVERNANCE AND REPRODUCTION

What this analysis can support

It can support

  • A reproducible chain of cleaning, RFM, probabilistic modelling and segmentation.
  • Temporal-holdout evidence about frequency error on data after the cutoff.
  • A first relationship-prioritisation view based on concentrated expected value.
  • Auditable artefacts: modular code, tests, bilingual notebooks, saved models and a Flask API.

It cannot support

  • That predicted revenue is margin or realised profit.
  • That a reactivation campaign causes the uplift implied by the forecast.
  • That permanent dropout captures all real-world reactivation behaviour.
  • That segments remain stable in another window or are production-ready.

Use decision: portfolio and investigation artefact. The recommended next step is to integrate campaign cost and margin, compare expected return by action and validate any policy with a controlled experiment.
