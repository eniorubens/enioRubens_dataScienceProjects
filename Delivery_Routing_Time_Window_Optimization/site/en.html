Fleet size first, distance second

The vehicle routing problem with time windows (VRPTW) determines which customers go on each route and the order in which they are served. The plan must respect vehicle capacity, customer time windows and the return to the depot.

Solomon is a synthetic academic benchmark. Each instance has 100 customers. Of the 56 instances across six families, 14 were set aside for development and 42 for the primary evaluation. SINTEF publishes the best known solutions under the same order of objectives.

  1. 1st · Minimize fleet sizeFind a feasible plan with the fewest vehicles.
  2. 2nd · Minimize distanceAmong plans with the same fleet size, prefer the shortest total Euclidean distance.

OR-Tools led the local methods

All three methods found feasible solutions in all 42 instances. The primary result uses the third of five OR-Tools replicates ordered by feasibility, fleet size and distance, with 120 seconds per replicate. The two deterministic baselines are nearest neighbor with time windows and a deadline-first greedy heuristic.

OR-Tools matched the fleet size in all 13 instances in the clustered C1 and C2 families. In R1 and RC1, it matched in just 1 of 15 instances. SINTEF remained ahead on the hardest cases.

How disruptions affect the plan

C1 tests fixed v1 evaluation plans under delays. C2 and C3 use the 14 development instances to simulate operating decisions. C1 compares the local heuristics, while C2 and C3 compare variants of the planning approach.

C1 · Delays under fixed plans

Each plan and scenario was simulated 1,000 times, with no replanning. When travel times increased by up to 10% per leg, service started after the window closed for 6.3% of OR-Tools customers, 9.3% with nearest neighbor and 21.1% with the greedy heuristic. Late depot returns were 13.4%, 32.9% and 43.3%, respectively.

When service times increased by up to 20%, 84.2% of OR-Tools routes had at least one late stop, versus 71.1% and 72.5%. A plan with fewer vehicles can have a lower share of late customers and a higher share of routes with a late stop.

C2 · Who gets served when vehicles are scarce

The scenario designated 20% of customers as high priority and tested a tenfold penalty for skipping them. The policy protected these customers while increasing the total number of customers left unserved. The heuristic varied across replicates, and the increases in total were small in this sample.

K is the fleet size of each instance's reference plan. In a separate scenario, limiting each vehicle to 80% of its capacity required a larger fleet in 6 of 14 instances.

The soft-window scenario was exploratory. Only 3 of 14 instances were solved with one fewer vehicle. Four were infeasible due to capacity, and seven failed to construct an initial solution.

C3 · Replanning with fewer vehicle changes

When 10% of customers were removed or inserted, searching from the prior plan preserved more route legs and reassigned fewer customers than starting from scratch. Even with that starting point, the median share of common customers who changed vehicles was about 27% to 30%.

Median reassignment between cold replicates was zero. For removals, 4 of 42 comparisons were above zero, with a maximum of 23.3%. For insertions, all were zero.

v2 changed fleet size in one instance

The post-hoc re-evaluation reused the same 42 previously seen instances. In RC207, fleet size dropped from 4 to 3 vehicles, bringing the number of SINTEF fleet matches from 24 to 25. Mean extra vehicles went from +0.50 to +0.48.

The internal scale changed from 100 to 10,000, and the fixed cost per vehicle changed from an absolute amount to one relative to a distance upper bound. The changes were applied together, so the RC207 result reflects the full v2 configuration.

The median equal-fleet gap went from +3.40% to +2.23%, with one extra instance in the subset. Of the 41 instances with the same fleet size across versions, 18 had distance changes within ±0.5%, 11 improved beyond that range and 12 worsened. v1 remains the primary evaluation.

Benchmark use only

production_eligible = false. The study uses Euclidean distances and synthetic windows. C1 simulates independent delays, while real traffic can affect multiple routes and times at once. A live operation needs road distances, actual fleet and measured service times, field validation, and replanning rules.
