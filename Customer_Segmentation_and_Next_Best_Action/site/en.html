01 · OVERVIEW

An analytical chain in three layers

The project starts with RFM, adds a temporal predictive layer and ends with a prescriptive simulation. The goal is relationship prioritization — not presenting simulated uplift as a causal effect.

01 · RFMRecency, frequency, monetary value and unique products.
02 · SegmentationK-Means with six behavioral profiles.
03 · PredictionProbability of repurchase in the future window.
04 · ActionIncentive selected by expected profit and budget.

Business question

Who deserves relationship priority, which action fits, and how should a limited budget be allocated?

Project answer

Combine historical value, inactivity risk, repurchase probability and incentive cost into an operational CRM export.

Main reading: segmentation concentrates value, but action recommendations depend on margin, cost and response assumptions. Those assumptions must remain hypotheses.

05 · PRESCRIPTIVE LAYER

Budget changes the recommended action

The simulation evaluates four actions — no incentive, low, medium and high — using adjusted probability, margin and cost. The budget heuristic exported 353 customers and used $2,895 out of a $10,000 limit.

06 · GOVERNANCE AND REPRODUCTION

What the project can support

It can support

  • A modular chain of RFM, segmentation, prediction and action.
  • Unequal revenue concentration across interpretable segments.
  • A materialized predictive hold-out and documented CRM export logic.
  • Auditable budget and action decisions through code parameters.

It cannot support

  • That simulated uplift is causal or experiment-validated.
  • That prescription beats a uniform campaign without reconciling scenarios.
  • Production readiness without monitoring and an A/B test.
  • That cluster names remain stable in another time window.

Use decision: portfolio and investigation artifact. Before operational use, review the objective function, validate incentive response with a controlled experiment and monitor drift.
