A two-month policy is not equivalent to a one-year policy

The freMTPL2freq dataset records how many claims occurred during each policy's coverage period. Since durations vary, counts must be related to exposure. This study compares rates as claims per 100 policy-years.

The evaluation question was direct: does a model that differentiates vehicle, driver, and regional profiles predict claim counts better than applying one annual rate across the portfolio?

Global rate

A single annual frequency estimated on training data and multiplied by each policy's exposure. This is the simple baseline.

Poisson

A profile-specific rate with log(Exposure) as an offset whose coefficient is fixed at 1. Expected count grows proportionally with coverage time.

NB2

A profile-specific rate with additional dispersion. On the main validation split with the full training set, it improved likelihood but lost on deviance and overall calibration.

Poisson reduced mean deviance on the test set

On the reserved test set, mean deviance was 0.31902 for Poisson and 0.33079 for the global rate. The 95% interval for the paired difference remained entirely below zero. The result also held when the bootstrap clustered records by covariate profile.

The confirmed improvement is in mean deviance. For overall calibration, the point absolute error was 0.050 for Poisson and 0.027 for the global rate. Each candidate's 95% interval for predicted − observed included zero. These intervals assess candidates separately and are not an equivalence test between models.

Very short exposures carry the largest error

The pattern observed on validation reappeared across all eight test bands: the fixed offset underestimated rates for short exposures and overestimated some longer exposures. The largest difference occurred in the first band, up to 0.02 year.

The test set was protected from modeling decisions

The 678,013 policies were assigned deterministically by hash to training, validation, and test. EDA and preprocessing were conducted on training. Candidates were compared on validation. The selected specification and final script were frozen before the single test execution.

  1. Training · 70%474,581 records for EDA, preprocessing, and fitting. Models were trained outside the notebooks, and their artifacts were saved once.
  2. Validation · 15%101,573 records to compare the global rate, Poisson, and NB2, assess zeros, and select the model.
  3. Test · 15%101,859 records opened once after 20 integrity checks. Only aggregated results were saved.

The PT-BR and EN-US notebooks load the same results and do not repeat model fits. Checksums, data contracts, the partition rule, the frozen model, and the final script make each result traceable.

The zeros did not require a second process

On validation, although about 95% of policies had no claims, the total number of zeros was consistent with the reference Poisson prediction. The interval for the difference between predicted and observed zeros included zero. The data contract also provided no documented hypothesis for a group of policies that was structurally risk-free. Therefore, ZIP and ZINB were not fitted.
